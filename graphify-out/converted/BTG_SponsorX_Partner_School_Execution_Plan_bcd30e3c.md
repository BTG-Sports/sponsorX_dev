# How to Use This Plan
This document converts the Partner School Package into a repeatable delivery system. The school and BTG should approve the annual calendar during kickoff, then use the monthly workplan, service playbooks and scorecard to manage execution.

## Operating Principles
- School approval comes first. All schedules, athletes, sponsors, content and campaigns move through designated school approval.
- Student-athlete safety and eligibility are non-negotiable. Parent/guardian consent, school policy and applicable athletic-association rules govern participation.
- Education precedes monetization. Athletes complete readiness training before being presented for sponsor opportunities.
- No guaranteed sponsorships or media placements. BTG develops opportunities and provides selected editorial exposure based on fit and approval.
- Document the work. Attendance, approvals, sponsor contacts, content releases and outcomes are recorded throughout the year.

# 4. Service-by-Service Execution Playbooks
## 4.1 School Onboarding and Program Planning
Purpose: Establish shared expectations, contacts, safeguards and a realistic calendar before athlete activity begins.
- Send welcome packet and data request within two business days of agreement.
- Hold a 60-minute kickoff with school leadership, athletics, communications and safeguarding contacts.
- Confirm priority sports, athlete selection method, blackout dates, available space and technology.
- Collect school brand rules, media rules, visitor procedures and applicable NIL/eligibility guidance.
- Issue the master calendar, responsibility matrix and approval path within five business days.

## 4.2 Four Athlete-Development Workshops
Purpose: Deliver a progressive learning sequence that moves athletes from awareness to practical readiness.
- Schedule four 75–90 minute sessions, ideally 6–8 weeks apart.
- Use sign-in, pre/post knowledge checks and one applied exercise per session.
- Provide a take-home worksheet and a specific action due before the next session.
- Offer one make-up method: recorded recap, virtual session or supervised worksheet.
- Report attendance, learning scores and follow-up needs within five business days.

## 4.3 Parent and Coach Information Session
Purpose: Align adults around expectations, safeguards, eligibility, time demands and family support.
- Host during Month 1 or 2, before public athlete profiles.
- Explain program scope, what is and is not guaranteed, approvals and communications.
- Review responsible social media, compensation basics, academic/team responsibilities and reporting concerns.
- Collect questions; publish an FAQ and escalation contact within three business days.

## 4.4 Athlete Profiles and Readiness Assessment
Purpose: Create accurate basic profiles and identify coaching required before sponsor consideration.
- Collect verified biography, sport, position/event, interests, achievements and contact routing.
- Audit social presence only with authorized access; do not request passwords.
- Score professionalism, communication, reliability, brand clarity, compliance awareness and support readiness.
- Assign status: Developing, Nearly Ready or Sponsor Ready.
- Review profile facts with athlete and school; refresh at least quarterly.

## 4.5 Athlete Media/Content Day
Purpose: Produce consistent, school-approved assets and teach athletes how to represent themselves.
- Approve date, location, shot list, interview prompts, dress code and releases four weeks ahead.
- Run timed stations: check-in, portrait, action/lifestyle, interview and sponsor-neutral clips.
- Use a naming convention and asset log; back up files the same day.
- Complete first review in 10 business days; route school/athlete approvals before use.
- Deliver approved selections and a 30-day publishing queue.

## 4.6 Local Business Sponsor Outreach
Purpose: Build a qualified community-business pipeline around school-aligned opportunities.
- Define excluded industries and sponsor criteria with the school.
- Research 30 prospects across food, health, retail, auto, finance, education and professional services.
- Prioritize proximity, community reputation, youth alignment, activation ability and budget fit.
- Use a three-touch sequence and log every contact in the CRM.
- Never promise athlete participation, sales, reach or exclusivity before written approval.

## 4.7 One School/Community Campaign
Purpose: Turn education and sponsor interest into one manageable, visible impact initiative.
- Select one concept against school priorities, athlete capacity, safety and sponsor fit.
- Create a one-page brief covering objective, audience, roles, deliverables, rights, budget and measurement.
- Secure approvals and agreements before production or public announcement.
- Run a production checklist and day-of command structure.
- Close with proof of delivery, results, thank-yous and lessons learned.

## 4.8 BTG Sports Talk / iMC Network Exposure
Purpose: Extend selected program stories through credible editorial coverage.
- Identify stories with educational, community or athlete-development value.
- Conduct editorial review; confirm facts, permissions and school timing.
- Prepare interview participants, talking points and contingency dates.
- Publish only approved assets; archive links and performance metrics.
- Separate editorial consideration from purchased sponsor rights.

## 4.9 Year-End Impact Report
Purpose: Provide a credible record of participation, activity, outreach, outcomes and lessons.
- Freeze data 10 business days after final activity.
- Validate attendance, readiness, sponsor activity, published content, campaign delivery and expenses.
- Collect school, athlete and sponsor feedback.
- Draft results, stories, gaps, recommendations and next-year options.
- Present within 30 days of program end and document renewal decision.

# 5. Workshop Curriculum
## Workshop 1 — NIL Awareness and Athlete Responsibility
Learning objective: Understand NIL basics, distinguish opportunity from risk and know when to ask for help.

### Core topics
- What NIL is and is not
- School/state/association rules and reporting
- Contracts, exclusivity, usage rights and conflicts
- Scams, prohibited categories and red flags
- Scenario exercise: accept, pause or decline

## Workshop 2 — Financial Literacy
Learning objective: Build habits for managing irregular income, taxes, saving and family expectations.

### Core topics
- Gross pay vs. take-home pay
- Simple 50/30/20 planning adapted for athletes
- Taxes and recordkeeping
- Banking, credit, fraud and professional advisors
- Budget exercise using a sample campaign payment

## Workshop 3 — Personal Branding
Learning objective: Define a credible identity that supports sport, school, community and career goals.

### Core topics
- Values, audience and authentic story
- Social profile audit and digital footprint
- Bio writing, content pillars and posting boundaries
- Sponsor fit and saying no
- Profile lab and peer feedback

## Workshop 4 — Media Readiness and Career Preparation
Learning objective: Communicate confidently in interviews and connect sports experience to future careers.

### Core topics
- Interview structure and message discipline
- On-camera presence and difficult questions
- Email, voicemail and meeting etiquette
- Resume/LinkedIn basics and transferable skills
- Recorded mock interview and feedback

# 6. Athlete Readiness and Safeguards
## Readiness Scoring Rubric


## Required Safeguards
- Roster verification and school eligibility acknowledgement.
- Parent/guardian consent for minors and athlete assent.
- Media release with permitted channels, term and revocation procedure.
- No direct one-to-one adult/minor communications outside approved channels.
- No athlete financial, medical, academic or family data included in sponsor materials unless specifically authorized.
- No sponsor use of school marks, uniforms or facilities beyond written permission.
- Written disclosure of deliverables, compensation, usage rights, term, exclusivity and cancellation.
- Incident reporting to the school and BTG safeguarding contacts on the same business day.
