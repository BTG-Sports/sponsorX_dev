Yes. I would make the pre-launch feel like athlete culture/content first and SponsorX advertising second. That gives you material to recruit athletes, demonstrate what SponsorX content looks like, and later show businesses exactly how they could fit into campaigns.
The first campaign series should progressively move from organic athlete participation → community engagement → subtle brand-ready content → SponsorX reveal.
# SponsorX Pre-Launch Campaign Series
### Campaign 1 — “WHAT’S YOUR PREGAME MEAL?”
Purpose: Food/restaurants/nutrition sponsors.
Question:
WHAT’S YOUR PREGAME MEAL?
Athletes record 15–30 second vertical videos:
“Before a game, I gotta have _____.”
Then:
“What’s yours?”
Content can show the meal, restaurant, homemade food, shake, etc.
CTA:
“Drop your pregame meal below.”
SponsorX opportunity: Restaurants, meal-prep companies, grocery stores, smoothie brands, nutrition companies.
Later this becomes:
WHAT’S YOUR PREGAME MEAL? — Presented by [Sponsor]

### Campaign 2 — “WHAT’S IN YOUR GAME-DAY BAG?”
Purpose: Apparel, footwear, accessories, wellness and sporting-goods sponsors.
Athlete empties their bag on camera.
Shoes
Headphones
Tape
Water bottle
Snacks
Recovery gear
Clothing
Personal item
Hook:
“Every athlete has that ONE thing they won't compete without. What's yours?”
This is particularly valuable because several brands can eventually activate around the same concept.

### Campaign 3 — “WHAT’S ON YOUR PREGAME PLAYLIST?”
Purpose: Build engagement and introduce the athlete's personality.
Ask:
“What are the 3 songs you HAVE to hear before you compete?”
Each athlete gives three.
BTG can turn the answers into:
THE SPONSORX PREGAME PLAYLIST
and publish it through BTG/iMC.
Sponsor opportunities later include headphones, wireless carriers, streaming/media, apparel and entertainment brands.

### Campaign 4 — “GAME-DAY FIT”
This should be very visual.
SHOW US YOUR GAME-DAY FIT.
Athletes show:
Arrival outfit → shoes → accessories → uniform.
Use a quick transition Reel.
Then ask fans:
RATE THE FIT: 1–10
This naturally creates opportunities for clothing stores, sneaker stores, jewelry/accessory companies, fashion brands and local boutiques.

### Campaign 5 — “THE $20 ATHLETE CHALLENGE”
This one gives SponsorX its first commerce-style campaign.
Question:
“You've got $20 to build the perfect post-game meal. What are you buying?”
Athlete goes into a restaurant/store.
Creates their $20 order.
Shows receipt/order.
Rates it.
This can eventually become:
### **THE $20 ATHLETE CHALLENGE
Presented by [Restaurant]**
And the sponsor could provide:
$5 OFF YOUR OWN $20 CHALLENGE
via SponsorX QR code.
Now you're demonstrating the future SponsorX model:
Content → Athlete → Fan → Offer → Purchase

### Campaign 6 — “WHO PUT YOU ON?”
This one has a different purpose.
It's about storytelling rather than products.
Ask every athlete:
“Who's the person that helped you get this far?”
Parent
Coach
Trainer
Teacher
Sibling
Mentor
15–45 second response.
Then tag that person if appropriate.
This creates emotional content and shows sponsors that SponsorX isn't simply athletes holding products.
Potential sponsors include banks, hospitals, education organizations, nonprofits, insurance companies and community-focused businesses.

### Campaign 7 — “IF YOU WEREN’T AN ATHLETE…”
Very simple:
“If you weren't playing your sport, what would you be doing?”
You'll get answers like:
Entrepreneur
Doctor
Musician
Engineer
Coach
Chef
Streamer
That opens another major SponsorX category:
career and education sponsorships.
Eventually:
BEYOND THE GAME CAREER SPOTLIGHT — Powered by [Company]

## I would run the sequence like this
The important thing is that the first seven campaigns shouldn't require sponsors. Run them with your existing athletes and the first athletes you recruit. They become your SponsorX proof-of-concept content.
Then when you approach a restaurant, you aren't explaining a hypothetical idea. You can show them “What's Your Pregame Meal?” and say:
“We want your restaurant to sponsor the next 10-athlete edition. We'll bring 10 local athletes into the campaign, distribute their content, create a fan offer and track the results through SponsorX.”
That's a much easier sale.
And I would make “What's Your Pregame Meal?” the official first SponsorX pilot campaign because it is inexpensive, easy for athletes to produce, relatable to fans, works across every sport, and creates an obvious path to your first restaurant sponsors.
