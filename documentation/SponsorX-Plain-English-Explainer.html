How SponsorX Works

The whole system, in plain English
Positioning and messaging reference  ·  September 2026  ·  Internal

The ten-second version

Local athletes already have your customers’ attention. SponsorX rents you that attention — as a package, at a fixed price, with proof it worked.

The thirty-second version

Sponsoring sports today gives you a banner and a shrug. You pay, your logo goes up, and nobody can tell you whether a single person walked through your door because of it.

SponsorX flips that. You tell us what you want and your budget. We pick a group of local athletes whose followers are your actual customers. They post, film, or show up in person. Fans scan a code to get an offer from you, and redeem it in your store. At the end you get a report showing how many people saw it, how many claimed the offer, and how many actually came in.

You never have to find an athlete, negotiate a rate, write a contract, or chase anyone for a deliverable. That is our job.

The problem, on both sides

For a business

One athlete with 20,000 followers is not worth the hassle of a contract. Ten of them is a real campaign — but now you have ten contracts, ten negotiations and ten people to chase. Nobody has time for that.

For an athlete

You have a real, engaged audience, but you are too small for a brand to bother with. So you get nothing, or you get a free pair of shoes.

SponsorX sits in the middle and bundles them. Many small audiences become one sellable thing, and one business deals with one invoice.

How it works, in five steps

  1. You say what you want. More foot traffic, a new product known, bodies at an event. Plus your budget.
  2. We pick the athletes. From our roster, matched to your neighbourhood and your customers. Not by follower count — by who actually gets engagement and who reliably delivers.
  3. They make the content. Posts, reels, appearances. You approve everything before it goes public.
  4. Fans get an offer. Each athlete gets their own code. A fan scans it, claims something — 20% off, a free drink — and redeems it with you.
  5. You get the numbers. Views, engagement, codes scanned, offers claimed, offers actually redeemed. Per athlete, so you know who moved the needle.
The one thing that makes it different

Step four. Every other form of local sponsorship ends at “your logo was seen.” This one ends at “1,870 people used your code.” That is the whole pitch. Everything else is a feature; this is the reason to believe.

The pitch to an athlete

  • You get paid for content you are half-making already
  • You pick each job. Nothing is automatic, and you can say no with no penalty
  • It is not exclusive. Your own deals stay yours. This is not signing your career over to an agency
  • Under 18? A parent or guardian has to approve before anything happens
  • You can see what you have earned and exactly where it stands

The four objections you will actually get

They askYou answer
“How much?” Start at $750. Three athletes, one post each, a real report. Deliberately cheap enough to say yes to without a meeting.
“Isn’t this just influencer marketing?” No. Influencers rent you an audience. Athletes rent you standing in a community. A kid people watch play on Friday night carries weight a random creator does not.
“How do I know it worked?” Because the offer is redeemed in your store. Not impressions. Redemptions.
“What if the athlete posts something stupid?” You approve every piece before it goes out. Nothing publishes without your sign-off.

Two things to be careful about

Do not lead with reach

The current homepage says “1.2M Monthly Reach / 2.4M Fans Engaged.” That is the old pitch — selling audience size, which invites comparison to buying ads, a game you lose on price. The current strategy sells outcomes. Lead with “$750, three athletes, and a number you can check.”

You have no results yet

Four anchor athletes, no completed campaigns. The first five campaigns are not sales, they are proof-building — price them as pilots and get written permission to publish the numbers. Until then, do not imply results you do not have. The case-study slots on the homepage are deliberately empty for exactly this reason.
